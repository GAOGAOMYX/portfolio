01 / Research reset

Fieldwork showed that the initial research question was too broad.

I began with a broad enquiry into practical digital capability in Glasgow's small independent businesses. Desk research and online audits could describe a topic, but they could not identify a specific service problem supported by evidence. Tutor feedback made the gap visible: I needed a defined participant group, direct contact and a way to separate observation from assumption.

I reset the method, mapped 145 street-facing units across Victoria Road and the High Street corridor, and completed two days of light-touch fieldwork. The mapping described an observable local context. It did not prove that every unit was independent or that they shared one operational problem.

What can the evidence support - and what must the design leave open?

02 / Case evidence

One owner case changed the design question from software adoption to responsibility.

The detailed case showed owners preparing invoices and VAT material themselves before an accountant entered records. Delegating more work was not automatically helpful: searching, explaining and answering follow-up questions could return work to the business in another form.

The opportunity became narrower and more useful: test whether a preparation role could absorb routine evidence handling without silently returning more checking and explanation work to the owner.

Whose time is the support system designed to save?

03 / Service response

Two matched configurations made the invisible work comparable.

Month-End Buffer sits before formal bookkeeping and accountancy. In the owner-led configuration, the owner organises material and receives every follow-up question. In the service-absorbed configuration, a preparation role applies fixed rules, records its evidence basis and returns only items that require business judgement.

The interfaces stay deliberately similar. The meaningful difference is who holds interruption, uncertainty and follow-up work.

  • Fictional emails, bank items and partner documents make the handover observable.
  • Items that still require business judgement are returned to the owner.
  • Every scenario states that it is a rehearsal, not accounting advice or a live service.

04 / Trial and iteration

The trial clarified what needed to be tested next.

One co-owner from the detailed case completed the service-absorbed route asynchronously. The participant could complete the sequence and allow fixed-rule handling without seeing every intermediate check, but did not support paying for a separate owner-facing service outside the accounting relationship.

The handoff language also remained unclear. The participant associated the proposal with an unfamiliar accounting tool instead of a responsibility model. I responded with a paper iteration clarifying what the service handles, when the owner is contacted and how the handoff relates to the accountant. That iteration remains untested.

Next validation gate

Test the clarified handoff, interview accounting practices about provision and liability, and examine whether a workable commercial relationship exists.

Evidence boundary

What this project does not claim

This project does not establish a Glasgow-wide need, validated business impact, commercial viability or accounting accuracy. It is argued from one detailed owner case and one unmoderated real-user run.
