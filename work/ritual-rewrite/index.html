Heritage learning service / 2025

Ritual Rewrite

A no-login browser game and printable teacher guide designed for short classroom sessions about gendered heritage.

Open working game↗
Role
Service Designer; built the browser game with AI coding assistance
Team
Five-person multidisciplinary team
Duration
Studio 1 project, 2025
Capabilities
Service design / Inclusive learning / Multidisciplinary collaboration / Interaction testing / Technical feasibility

01 / Context and opportunity

The service had to translate gendered heritage into a short classroom activity.

The project explored how gendered rituals and invisible labour associated with the historic Willow Tea Rooms could become meaningful learning for Scottish secondary students. It also needed to connect heritage interpretation with future careers in hospitality.

The challenge was not just to turn historical content into a game. The team had to balance conceptual depth, short attention windows, teacher workload, accessibility and device constraints.

02 / Learning from failure

Early prototype failures clarified the interaction requirements.

A physical Tea-Time Bias Bingo activity failed because its categories were interpreted inconsistently. An early digital health-point mechanic created urgency but could overwhelm the learning purpose. Long historical paragraphs could not simply be transferred into short cards.

The failures showed that the team needed enough tension to prompt discussion, while still allowing students to pause, read and interpret. Content length, tone and interaction purpose had to become part of the service specification.

03 / My contribution

I connected the classroom journey with the browser game.

I helped design the teacher launch-stage journey and reject mechanics that did not fit the learning context, and I built the HTML and JavaScript game with AI coding assistance.

Browser testing revealed service-design issues. Fast-click, touch-and-scroll and score-overflow failures showed how quickly the experience could lose its learning purpose. I translated those failures into interaction rules and argued for a lightweight no-login web route instead of a Unity-only experience.

  • Teacher launch and reflection moments were treated as part of the service.
  • The browser game reduced account, installation and device barriers.
  • A printable guide gave teachers control over framing and discussion.

04 / Final service

The final outcome combines a browser game with printed teacher support.

Students enter through the browser game, interpret ritual cards and make choices. Teachers introduce the historical context, manage discussion and use the printed support to connect the activity with critical reflection and future hospitality roles.

This was a feasibility decision as much as a visual one: teacher autonomy and easy access mattered more than adding complex technical features.

Next validation gate

Test with secondary-school students and teachers, then refine content specifications, accessibility and curriculum fit.

Evidence boundary

What this project does not claim

The prototype was tested with peers in a simulated classroom, not with the intended students or teachers. It cannot claim improved learning, accessibility validation or curriculum impact.
