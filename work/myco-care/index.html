Myco-Care

A concept for neighbours to exchange help through a community centre and stairwell lockers, with rules borrowed from how fungal networks share resources. It is a concept study and has not been tested with residents.

01 / Problem framing

The study treats urban isolation as a systems problem.

What if community support behaved less like one-way charity and more like a resilient exchange network? Older residents may have time and local knowledge but face physical limitations; younger residents may have mobility and digital fluency but lack time and local belonging. Traditional goodwill models can also create friction and burnout when value travels only one way.

The design question became how a community could sense needs, route help and recover when one hub fails - without relying on a central property manager.

Move from unsustainable charity to resilient symbiosis.

02 / Biological decoding

Three biological behaviours became three service rules.

Mycorrhizal networks distribute resources through many connected nodes. The design translated pressure into priority, mutual exchange into two-way value and network redundancy into service recovery.

  • Pressure = priority: urgent needs receive greater signal intensity.
  • Exchange, not charity: every participant can give and receive different forms of value.
  • Redundancy: if the main hub closes, local stairwell touchpoints keep the path available.

03 / System concept

A community centre and stairwell lockers provide two levels of access.

The main community centre coordinates the network and refers residents to professional support when needed. Shared stairwell lockers let residents signal needs and exchange items at different times, without entering one another's homes.

Older residents contribute time, stories, monitoring and local knowledge. Younger residents contribute physical help and technical support. A simple credit system recognises exchange without turning the relationship into cash payment.

Critical next step

The concept needs field research with residents, housing providers and community organisations before any service or hardware proposal can be treated as viable.

Evidence boundary

What this project does not claim

Myco-Care is a systems and biomimicry concept study. It was not co-designed or field-tested with the communities, housing providers or organisations represented in the model.
