Protection Preview

People were stopping before they asked for a life-insurance quote. With Scottish Widows, our five-person team interviewed members of the public and built a website that explains cover, buying and claiming first, with no quote and no form. I designed one of the two prototypes behind it.

01 / Final group outcome

The final website explains protection before introducing a quote.

Protection Preview is a stand-alone information experience before the quote journey. It lets people explore how premiums and cover relate, what a payout could mean, the policy lifecycle, the claim journey and the buying process - without first entering personal details.

The final website does not provide quotes or collect personal details. Users can explore, pause or leave at any point. It combines Prototype 1's stand-alone exploration with the process visibility developed in my Prototype 2.

  • Interactive premium and cover example with a clear illustrative-data note.
  • Real-life value, cover types and additional services are explained in layers.
  • Buying and claim stages are shown before commitment is requested.
  • The closing screen reinforces user control: You do not need to decide today.

02 / Research and reframe

The problem was not simply that protection needed simpler words.

My first interpretation treated protection as a communication problem. Stakeholder feedback challenged that assumption: pricing depends on personal circumstances, while underwriting and claims cannot responsibly be reduced to one simple promise.

Public engagement identified different reasons for disengagement - cost, relevance, family responsibility, uncertainty about claiming, discomfort around health history and fear of losing control in a form-led journey. The critical moment was before the quote, when interest is present but clarity and confidence are still unstable.

03 / My individual contribution

Prototype 2 showed the buying and claiming process step by step.

Inside the team project, I owned Prototype 2: its user flow, page architecture, visual and interaction design, browser testing and iteration after feedback. I built it in HTML, CSS and JavaScript with AI coding assistance and published it on GitHub Pages.

I worked from an information architecture to low-fidelity wireframes in Figma, and took the wireframes to our tutor's weekly session and to another student team before building the browser version. I then presented the browser version to another student team and to Scottish Widows staff.

The prototype turns an unfamiliar journey into visible stages. It gave the final group direction its process clarity, while testing showed that the experience still felt too close to starting a quote.

04 / Iteration and evaluation

The final website combined the strengths of both prototype directions.

Peer and stakeholder feedback showed that the overall direction was understandable, but content accuracy, visual scanning and concrete examples needed work. The team strengthened product definition, information accuracy, content structure and visual guidance.

A second round of testing found that the purpose was clear, while the connection to the brand, concrete scenarios and personalisation needed more work.

What changed in my practice

For sensitive or regulated services, I now treat clarity as showing necessary detail at the right time, explaining why it matters and allowing the user to pause or leave.

Evidence boundary

What this project does not claim

This was a research and learning prototype, not a deployed Scottish Widows service. It did not use real personalised prices and cannot support conversion, commercial, underwriting or customer-impact claims.
